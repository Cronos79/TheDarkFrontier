THE DARK FRONTIER
Vision Document
Version 2.0
1. Vision

The Dark Frontier is a medieval fantasy city builder focused on creating a living settlement rather than commanding armies.

The player begins with a small expedition on the edge of an untamed frontier. Starting with only a wagon, a handful of settlers, and limited supplies, they must establish a permanent settlement capable of surviving an increasingly dangerous world.

Every building placed, every resource gathered, and every citizen assigned contributes to the long-term growth of the settlement. The player's success is measured not by conquering enemies, but by building a thriving civilization that can survive generations.

The game emphasizes planning, logistics, production chains, specialization, and gradual expansion while maintaining a dangerous and mysterious world where magic and dragons exist—but are exceptionally rare.

The Dark Frontier is designed as a long-term colony simulation where every decision shapes the future of the settlement.

2. Core Design Pillars
City Builder First

The settlement is the main character.

Construction, logistics, production, roads, citizens, and resource management always take priority over combat.

Combat exists to support the city—not replace it.

Living Simulation

Citizens are individuals.

Every citizen has:

A home
A workplace
A daily schedule
Needs
Responsibilities

The settlement should feel alive whether the player is watching or not.

Meaningful Progression

Progress comes from building a better settlement, not waiting for research timers.

The settlement learns through experience.

Foresters become better by cutting trees.

Masons become better by quarrying stone.

Farmers become better through harvests.

Soldiers improve through training and experience.

The player's choices determine the settlement's identity.

Data Driven

Gameplay should be driven by data whenever practical.

Buildings

Resources

Recipes

Production

Roads

Storage

Citizens

Progression

should all be configurable through data rather than requiring unique code whenever possible.

Rare Fantasy

Magic exists.

Dragons exist.

Ancient civilizations existed.

None of them are common.

Most settlements may never see a dragon.

Most citizens will never cast magic.

These systems should feel legendary rather than routine.

3. The Player Experience

The player begins with:

One Expedition Wagon
A small group of settlers
Limited supplies
An unexplored frontier

The first goal is simple.

Survive.

Establish resource production.

Build permanent homes.

Create food production.

Found a real settlement.

As the settlement grows, new opportunities naturally appear.

Specialized industries.

Trade.

Military.

Exploration.

Magic.

Eventually the player transforms a temporary camp into a prosperous medieval city.

4. Early Game Philosophy

The first fifteen minutes teach the player the core gameplay loop.

The player begins with access to only the essential buildings.

Lumber Camp
Stone Quarry
Roads

After establishing both resource industries, additional buildings become available.

Houses
Herbalist
Town Hall

This progression teaches the economy naturally without relying on scripted tutorials.

Every new building introduces another layer of the simulation.

5. Resource Economy

The economy grows in layers.

Tier 1

Basic survival resources.

Wood
Stone
Food

These support the first settlement.

Tier 2

Early civilization.

Examples include:

Clay
Sand
Leather

These unlock improved construction and manufacturing.

Tier 3

Mining and industry.

Examples include:

Iron
Copper
Coal

These support advanced tools, weapons, and production.

Tier 4

Refined industry.

Examples include:

Steel
Bricks
Glass
Flour
Bread

Production chains become increasingly specialized.

Tier 5

Legendary resources.

Examples include:

Mana Crystals
Dragon Bone
Dragon Scale
Ancient Stone
Dark Essence

These resources are rare and tied to late-game content.

6. Mastery Progression

The Dark Frontier does not use a traditional research system.

Instead, settlements improve by doing.

Every major profession has its own Mastery branch.

Examples include:

Forestry
Masonry
Agriculture
Military
Trade
Craftsmanship
Magic
Dragon Lore

Performing related activities advances that profession.

Examples:

Cutting trees improves Forestry.

Mining stone improves Masonry.

Harvesting crops improves Agriculture.

Trading goods improves Commerce.

Training soldiers improves Military.

Mastery unlocks:

New buildings
Better tools
Improved production
Settlement bonuses
New professions
Specialized industries

Players are never locked into one path.

A settlement can specialize early while expanding into additional branches later.

Every city develops its own unique identity.

7. Roads and Infrastructure

Roads form the backbone of every settlement.

Roads are used to connect buildings into a functioning community.

Future systems may reward connected infrastructure through improved logistics, movement, and production efficiency.

As settlements expand, infrastructure becomes as important as individual buildings.

8. Military

The military exists to defend the settlement.

The player commands a relatively small professional force rather than massive RTS armies.

Examples include:

Guards
Archers
Patrols
Towers
Walls
Mercenaries

Military strength allows safer expansion into dangerous territory.

Players who neglect defense may rely on hired mercenaries while focusing on economic specialization.

9. Magic

Magic is intentionally rare.

Magic should never replace the city's economy.

Instead it provides unique opportunities, difficult choices, and powerful late-game options.

Magic should always feel valuable because of its rarity.

10. Dragons

Dragons are legendary creatures.

They should inspire excitement, fear, and curiosity.

Some may threaten settlements.

Others may become allies.

Many players may spend dozens of hours before encountering one.

Dragons should always remain memorable.

11. Long-Term Gameplay

As settlements mature, players naturally choose different priorities.

Examples include:

A forestry settlement exporting lumber.

A mining settlement exporting stone and metal.

An agricultural settlement feeding neighboring cities.

A military settlement protecting trade routes.

A trading settlement importing raw materials and exporting manufactured goods.

No single strategy should dominate every playthrough.

Player choices should create unique stories.

12. Development Philosophy

Gameplay comes first.

Prototype quickly.

Build strong systems before adding polish.

Placeholder art is acceptable during Alpha if it clearly communicates gameplay.

Features should be designed to scale rather than rewritten repeatedly.

Every major system should support future expansion while remaining simple enough to iterate during development.

The goal is not to build the largest city builder.

The goal is to build one where every system works together to create a believable, living frontier settlement.
